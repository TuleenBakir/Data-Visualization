Peak demand: Friday 18:00 at Zone 161

The single busiest hour is Thursday 18:00 with 122,673 trips. The top 3 hour×zone combos are all Friday evening at Zone 161 (Midtown Center): 18:00→46,268, 17:00→45,353, 19:00→39,022 trips.

Solo riders dominate

66.6% of non-Flex trips carry exactly 1 passenger. Pairs (2 pax) account for 22.6%. Groups of 3+ represent only 10.8% combined. Occupancy data is unavailable for the 17.3% Flex Fare trips.

Airport zones command premium fares

Zone 132 (JFK) leads on total revenue at $31.27M. Zone 161 (Midtown Center) leads on trip volume. Distance↔fare correlation is 0.728 (Pearson r) — the strongest operational predictor of fare.

CBD fee: minimal fare impact

Avg fare dropped from $18.12 (pre Jan 5) to $17.53 (post Jan 5) — a 3.25% decline — driven by ride-mix shift rather than the fee itself. The pre-period covers only 4 days (New Year skew).

How temporal, geographic, and policy factors drive trip volume and rider behaviour — 14 nodes · 18 edges

Fare Formation: 4 key causal chains

Zone → Rate Code → Fare → Total; Distance → Duration → Fare → Total; Payment Type → Tip → Total; Post-Jan-5 Policy → CBD Fee → Total. All seven fee components converge on total_amount.

Policy has direct & indirect demand impact

CBD policy directly suppresses trip demand (post-Jan-5 effect), which cascades into lower zone revenue and reduced road congestion — demonstrating mediation through the demand node.

Payment type is jointly caused by zone & hour

Zone type and hour of day both independently cause payment mix. Credit card adoption enables recorded tips, while fare magnitude also drives tip level — creating a fare → tip → revenue indirect path.

DAG interpretation

Each arrow encodes a hypothesised direct cause, not mere correlation. Nodes can act as mediators (e.g., Fare Amount) or colliders. Variables without a direct arrow may still correlate through shared common causes upstream.

Key Insights

Consolidated findings across all notebooks — OW Yellow Caps · NYC Yellow Taxi Q1 2025

Most Actionable Findings
1

Airport routes earn 3× more per trip

JFK (Zone 132) averages $72.42/trip vs Midtown's $23.67/trip. LaGuardia (Zone 138) earns $67.50/trip. Optimising driver dispatch toward airports unlocks outsized revenue per kilometre driven.

2

Thu–Fri 17:00–19:00 is the single peak window

Thursday 18:00 alone generates 122,673 trips fleet-wide. The top 3 hour × zone combos (Zone 161, Fri 17–19:00) each exceed 39,000 trips. Surge pricing and driver incentives should concentrate here.

3

March demand is 19% higher than January

Jan = 3.48M · Feb = 3.58M · Mar = 4.15M source trips. The seasonal ramp is consistent across every day of the week and every hour — Q2 capacity should scale proportionately.

4

CBD fee absorbed with minimal fare impact

Average total fell just 2.06% post-Jan-5 ($27.14 → $26.58). All zone-level fare changes are within ±3%. The fee is not a material pricing risk; the pre-period skew is driven by 4-day New Year holiday data.

5

Cash tips are structurally invisible

Cash accounts for 10.2% of trips (~1.07M). Tip amount is recorded only for credit card transactions ($3.92 median). Revenue reporting underestimates real rider generosity for every cash trip.

6

Vendor data quality needs monitoring

7.86% of raw rows (879.8K) were dropped. Negative fares alone accounted for 535,497 rows (4.78% of raw data) — predominantly a vendor submission issue requiring upstream correction.

8 · Causal Structure (DAG Findings)
Figure 11A — Fare Formation DAG · 14 nodes · 19 edges
Zone → Rate Code → Fare Amount → Total Amount
Distance → Duration → Fare Amount → Total Amount
Payment Type → Tip Amount → Total Amount
Post-Jan-5 Policy → CBD Fee → Total Amount

All 7 fee components (Fare, Tip, Congestion Surcharge, CBD Fee, Tolls, Airport Fee, MTA Surcharge) funnel into total_amount. Zone is the most influential upstream node — it causally determines Rate Code, Distance profile, Toll eligibility, Airport Fee eligibility, and Congestion eligibility simultaneously.

Figure 11B — Demand Driver DAG · 14 nodes · 18 edges
Hour / Day-of-Week / Month → Trip Demand → Zone Revenue
Zone Type → Trip Distance → Fare Amount → Zone Revenue
Demand → Road Congestion → Trip Duration → Trip Speed
CBD Policy → Reduced Demand (post Jan-5 direct effect)
Zone + Hour → Payment Mix → Tip Rate

Trip Demand acts as a mediator: policy effects on revenue are partially indirect (policy → demand → revenue). Fare Amount is a convergence point fed by both Distance and Duration — making it the central variable for revenue prediction.
